2  Summary Statistics

Bali, Engle, and Murray (2016), Chapter 2, summarize the distribution of stock characteristics within each year, then average those statistics across years. This gives each year’s cross-section equal weight.

2.1 Implementation

2.1.1 Periodic Cross-Sectional Summary Statistics

The sample covers U.S. common stocks at each December 31 from 1988 through 2012. We take the December value of the 12-month daily beta from the market beta chapter. It uses all available daily excess returns in the calendar year and requires at least 200 matched observations.

import polars as pl

from era_pl import load_parquet

annual_beta = (
    load_parquet("betas", "eap")
    .with_columns(year=pl.col("month").dt.year())
    .filter(pl.col("month").dt.month() == 12, pl.col("year").is_between(1988, 2012))
    .select("permno", "year", Beta="12M")
)

For each year, compute the statistics across the stocks with a beta. We use the sample standard deviation, bias-adjusted skewness and excess kurtosis, and linearly interpolated percentiles. The count is the number of available values in that cross-section.

def cross_section(variable):
    x = pl.col(variable)
    return [
        x.mean().alias("Mean"),
        x.std().alias("SD"),
        x.skew(bias=False).alias("Skew"),
        x.kurtosis(fisher=True, bias=False).alias("Kurt."),
        x.min().alias("Min"),
        x.quantile(.05, interpolation="linear").alias("5%"),
        x.quantile(.25, interpolation="linear").alias("25%"),
        x.median().alias("Median"),
        x.quantile(.75, interpolation="linear").alias("75%"),
        x.quantile(.95, interpolation="linear").alias("95%"),
        x.max().alias("Max"),
        x.count().alias("n"),
    ]

annual_stats = (
    annual_beta.group_by("year")
    .agg(cross_section("Beta"))
    .sort("year")
    .collect()
)

2.1.2 Average Cross-Sectional Summary Statistics

To describe the typical cross-section, take the mean of each column in Table 2.1. For example, if \(n_t\) stocks have valid betas in year \(t\), their mean is \(\bar{\beta}_t = \sum_{i=1}^{n_t}\widehat{\beta}_{i,t}/n_t\). The table reports \(T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bar{\beta}_t\). A pooled mean would instead give years with more observations more weight. The same two-step procedure applies to the median and other statistics.

average_stats = annual_stats.select(pl.exclude("year").mean())

In this build the average annual mean beta is 0.75 and the average annual median is 0.71, both matching BEM at the reported precision. The average cross-section has 5,195 estimates, compared with 5,198 in the book. The annual table also shows that mean beta increased from 0.46 in 1988 to 1.04 in 2012. The time-series average hides that change.

2.2 Presentation and Interpretation

We also summarize year-end market capitalization in millions of dollars, its natural logarithm (size), book-to-market, and the following calendar year’s excess return. The size chapter exports December market capitalization and its log. The value chapter exports the ratio of prior-year fiscal book equity to prior December market capitalization. We use its signed ratio, consistent with the negative observations in BEM Table 2.3.

We compound available CRSP monthly stock returns within the holding year and subtract the compounded risk-free return. This construction uses CRSP’s reported monthly returns. BEM §1.1 does not spell out how its annual returns handle delistings. Formation years run through 2012, but the return series ends in 2012, so the last formation year contributes zero observations to the return count and no return moments.

Apply the same summary to each variable separately. This keeps stocks with a beta but no book-to-market ratio in the beta row. The observation count reflects the available data for each variable.

variables = ["Beta", "MktCap", "Size", "BM", "excess"]
all_stats = (
    annual_panel.unpivot(on=variables, index="year", variable_name="Measure")
    .group_by("year", "Measure")
    .agg(cross_section("value"))
    .group_by("Measure")
    .agg(pl.exclude("year", "Measure").mean())
)

Market capitalization has a long right tail. In Table 2.3, the mean is about $2 billion while the median is about $188 million. Taking logs reduces skewness. Average next-year excess returns are 12.40% per year, matching BEM, with a much smaller median of about 0.90%. Book-to-market averages 0.74 across 4,336 stocks per year, versus 0.71 across 4,273 in BEM. The average yearly maximum excess return is 1,918% here and 1,841% in the book.
