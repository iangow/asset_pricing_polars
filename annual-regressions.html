6  Fama and Macbeth Regression Analysis

This chapter uses BEM’s annual methodology sample from Chapter 2 to illustrate the two steps of a Fama–MacBeth regression. The first step fits a cross-sectional regression in each formation year. The second averages the coefficients across years and estimates their standard errors from the time series of annual coefficients.

import polars as pl

from eap_pl import build_annual_panel

annual_panel = build_annual_panel()

6.1 Implementation

6.1.1 Periodic Cross-Sectional Regressions

Compare three univariate regressions with a regression that includes beta, size, and book-to-market together. The outcome is the next year’s excess return in percent. Following Bali et al. (2016, chap. 6), winsorize the regressors at 0.5% in each year’s regression sample. Use six Newey–West lags when estimating standard errors for the averages.

import statspai as sp

specifications = [["Beta"], ["Size"], ["BM"], ["Beta", "Size", "BM"]]
term_labels = {"BM": "Book-to-market"}
fits = []
for terms in specifications:
    sample = (
        annual_panel.select("year", "excess", *terms).drop_nulls()
        .with_columns(
            pl.col(terms).era.winsorize(prob=0.005, quantile_method="linear").over("year")
        )
    )
    fits.append(sp.fama_macbeth("excess ~ " + " + ".join(terms),
                               data=sample, time="year", lags=6))

6.2 Interpreting FM Regressions

The ordinary Fama–MacBeth standard error is the standard deviation of the annual slopes divided by the square root of the number of years. For the size slope in the regression with all three characteristics, compare the ordinary \(t\)-statistic with the six-lag Newey–West \(t\)-statistic.

size_slopes = periodic[-1]["Size"]
ordinary_se = size_slopes.std() / len(size_slopes)**0.5
pl.DataFrame({
    "Standard error": ["Ordinary", "Newey–West (six lags)"],
    "t": [size_slopes.mean() / ordinary_se, fits[-1].tvalues["Size"]],
}).with_columns(pl.col("t").round(2))
